How to read thisEach agent is a machine, and stronger agents get bigger machines. The amber floor is the lead: one machine, Claude, running each job on Opus or (for checkpoints) Sonnet; the lit plate shows which; the green floor is a developer. Anything that changes code goes through Checks; directions and findings are text, so they go straight on. Each ticket is a box: it waits in the queue, moves to the agent working on it, and ends in Merged. Each belt is one path a ticket can take, named on the belt. A belt moves only while a ticket travels on it. When Claude reaches a usage limit its machine goes grey, and tickets waiting for it line up in red on its track. Hover or click a box to see the ticket; the tickets it needs get a yellow outline. The Dependencies tab shows the whole chain.

Work flows top to bottom. Each arrow points from a ticket to one that needs it, so a ticket always sits below everything it waits for, one row after the last of them. Lines join neighbouring steps only; a ticket that also waits for something further up says so in yellow ("also after 42"). Links implied by others are left out (if 25 needs 24 and 24 needs 21, there is no 21→25). Under each title, one mark per job shows who worked on it and in what order, up to the slider's time. Hover a box to light up what it needs and what it unblocks. The slider and play below work here too.

Production timeline

One lane per machine, one block per job (the number is the ticket). A red top edge is a failed attempt. Red hatching is a Claude usage limit. Idle stretches over 40 min are drawn shorter, with their real length written on them, so nights don't take the whole width.
