In short

I couldn't measure the writes from here, so this plan measures first and then fixes the most likely cause.

  1. 1
    Is it bad for the SSD?It depends on how much is written per day. A typical consumer 1 TB SSD is guaranteed for about 600 TB written; depending on the model that is anywhere from about 150 to over 1,000 TB. A few TB in total is under 1% of that, which is nothing to worry about. A few TB every week would wear out a 600 TB drive in a few years, which is worth fixing. The drive keeps its own wear figures, and those settle it.→ Open the Disks app, pick the SSD, open its menu and choose 'SMART Data & Self-Tests'. Send me 'Percentage used' (or 'Data units written') and say what period your system monitor's terabytes cover.Comment…
  2. 2
    Space is fine; it's the writesI checked the space today. /tmp holds 19.8 MB, the factory's temp folders 0.5 MB, the run logs 1.5 GB and the metrics database 14 MB. But free space doesn't show what was written and then deleted again, and that is what wears an SSD.→ Ticket f305-01: 'softfield look disk' shows GB written since boot and GB per day.Comment…
  3. 3
    The likely cause: the tests the factory runs again and againSince 12 November the line has run 20 to 24 hours on most days, with 65 to 116 jobs a day. Every check, by the gate or a developer, runs the whole test suite. That creates thousands of small git repos, databases and browser profiles and deletes them minutes later. On Ubuntu /tmp sits on the SSD, so all of it really gets written. Before F175's fix one run left about 2 GB behind. Run that many times a day and it plausibly adds up to terabytes a month. Other possible writers I'll check: Docker rebuilding a project's app each time main moves, the review's pictures, and swap.→ f305-02 starts by measuring how much of each day's writes come from the checks.Comment…
  4. 4
    Is it needed? Mostly not.The tests have to run, but their temporary files don't have to touch the SSD. They live for minutes and are deleted at the end of each job, so they can sit in memory. If the measurement confirms the checks are the main writer, the factory's temp folder moves to /dev/shm, only when there are at least 4 GB free there.→ Ticket f305-02, after f305-01.Comment…
  5. 5
    Better than making all of /tmp memoryYou could also switch the whole of /tmp to memory, as newer Ubuntu releases do. That is a system change for you to make, and with LM Studio loaded a large /tmp could push the machine into swap, which writes to the SSD again.→ Nothing, unless you'd rather make all of /tmp memory yourself.Comment…
