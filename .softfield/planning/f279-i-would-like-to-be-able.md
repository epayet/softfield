> 1. **Is it worth a new page? Yes, and it isn't overkill**: At 20:40 the queue held 145 tickets and 2 jobs across 4 projects: softfield's 141 tickets, travel-companion's 3, coucou's 1 and wardrobe-teller's setup job. None of today's pages can show that list. The Overview has room for a few rows, and on a phone 145 would bury Now. The board shows cards rather than tickets, one project at a time. Tickets is also one project at a time, with the latest work first. A page on its own is cheap: one route and one template. I'd leave it out of the tabs (the menu is already being cut down in F194, The menu: Overview, Planning, Board in fixed places). You reach it from the Overview's Queue panel and from the map.
>    Action: f279-03 builds `/queue` from the mock-up.
> 2. **Part of the map? Linked from it, not inside it**: The map in F199 (The factory floor: a small strip on the Overview and a one-screen map) is meant to fit on one screen with few words; F199 D5 moved the words onto taps. A list of 145 rows would break that. What the map gets is a link: tapping its queue station gives the count and "The whole queue ›". The new page is drawn in the factory theme, with a belt of the next six crates at the top, so it still looks like part of the floor.
>    Action: f279-05, once F199's taps (f199-04) are built.
> 3. **Worked out at each pick, by the runner itself**: You're right that the dry run is the key. Today there are two orders: the runner picks with `choose_ticket`, while the dashboard rebuilds the order on its own (`queries.run_order`) and ignores Claude's limits. So the Overview can say something the runner won't do: that's set-aside bug-42, and bug-21 is the dry run getting jobs held by Claude wrong. In the plan, the runner works out the whole factory's list at each pick and stores it. The page, the Overview and `softfield run --dry-run` all show that stored list, so they can't disagree. The cost: during a long lead job (up to 2.5 h this week) the list is the one from the start of that job. That's still what the runner acts on next. Anything you approve or reorder meanwhile shows on the page as on its way, applied at the next pick.
>    Action: f279-01 (the lead's: the runner's code is protected) takes in bug-21. bug-42 has nothing left to fix once f279-04 lands, so I'll remove both then.
> 4. **The sentence that explains the order**: The runner writes it, using exactly what it uses to pick. The first rule that applies wins: you paused the factory; main fails its tests; Claude is at its limit until a given time (developers only); over pace or past a mark (developers first, the lead when nothing else can); the fast lane goes first; otherwise your board's order. Up to three facts follow. Tonight's version: "Over pace: developers first, the lead when nothing else can. Claude's week 82% used, today's pace 42%. 2 developer tickets can go now, about 35 min."
>    Action: Decision D4, built in f279-01.
> 5. **Found while checking: over pace is decided one project at a time**: The runner asks each project for a ticket in turn. If a project has nothing for developers, its first lead ticket fills the gap, even when another project has a developer ticket that can start. So once travel-companion's m8-08-trip-by-hand is done, its lead ticket m5-01-remove-memory-place-row goes before softfield's developer ticket f269-01. That spends Claude while over pace, which goes against ADR 50 item 2 ("when nothing else can start"). I read this from the code (`project_round`, `choose_ticket`). I couldn't watch it happen from here. Fixing it keeps an accepted ADR rather than changing a rule, so it doesn't need a new ADR. If you'd rather your project order beat the pace, say so and I'll drop the ticket.
>    Action: f279-02 (the lead's, tests first): over pace, a lead ticket fills the gap only when no project has anything else that can start.
