## Sign-off sheet

### 1. What changed
- **The Overview is the factory line.** It opens on **Now** (what is being worked on, with its goal), then the allowances, then the floor drawn from the field to the harvest stash: one machine per agent with a LOCAL or CLOUD plate and its rating, three blinking lights and smoke while it works, belts with today's count that roll yellow while they carry, and a checks light that flashes green or red when a result lands. Everything stays still under "reduce motion".
- **On a phone** the same floor is drawn top to bottom, readable at 390 px.
- **Tap any crate** for a card: the ticket, its goal, who works on it and for how long, what it waits for, and a link to its page.
- **Replay** a stretch ("since you last looked", the last hour, today, the last 24 h): Play, ◀ ▶ and three speeds; "While you were away" says in one line what got merged, failed or escalated, and lists the merged tickets.
- **What clogged the line today:** minutes waiting for Claude's limit, failed attempts, idle machines, stopped for you, and the biggest of them.
- **The factory look** everywhere: dark by default, light when your system asks, fonts served by the dashboard itself.
- **The runner:** stopping it (Ctrl-C, a kill) now stops the agent it started, and `runner run` reviews a milestone by itself once all its tickets are done (this review is the first).
- Mum sees nothing new: F3 is factory-only.

### 2. How to test it yourself
1. `uv run runner dashboard --host <this machine's Wi-Fi address>` and open it on the desktop: the top shows Now, then the allowances, then the floor.
2. While the runner works, watch for a minute: the floor shouldn't flicker, a carrying belt keeps rolling, and the checks light flashes when a result lands.
3. Click a crate in the queue, then the one on a working machine: each opens its card; click outside to close it. Leave a card open across a refresh: its minutes move on.
4. Pick "the last hour" in the replay bar, then go back to the list: "since you last looked" still shows the time you opened the page, not a few seconds ago (fixed by this review).
5. Press Replay, then Play: crates move event by event and the words under the bar say what happened. "Back to live" returns to now.
6. On your phone, on the same Wi-Fi: the floor is drawn top to bottom, every word readable, and tapping a crate opens its card. Turn the phone: the desktop floor appears.
7. Scroll to "While you were away" after a night's run: each ticket takes a full line (fixed by this review; it was squeezed into a narrow column).
8. Switch your system to light mode: the dashboard follows.

The lessons that mattered:
1. **The line's drawing was beyond the local developers** (2 of 16 first time; Opus finished most). What changed: the plan's rule is now "one small, picture-backed change on top of a lead-written core" (lead.md, Known range).
2. **A fix ticket whose `files:` are only tests can't pass `tests-fail-on-main`.** Four of them each cost three attempts and an escalation. What changed: pre-flight now sends such a ticket to the lead before any attempt.
3. **"Read for 10 minutes, no edit" was the commonest failure** (11 in one session). The 10-minute stop (F2) kept it cheap; the tickets that hit it pointed at long files without line numbers (task-writing.md, "Lessons from F3").
4. **Fix tickets fix the line they're told about, not its neighbours,** and the checkpoint after f4-35 passed a half-fixed XSS. What changed: this review rebuilt the card without HTML; lead.md's Known range says it.
5. **Skipped tests hide regressions:** ADR 21.
